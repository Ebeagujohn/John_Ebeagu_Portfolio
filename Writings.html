AI Dependence as Violation of Kantian Rational Sovereignty and Sartrean Authenticity

2026 ResearcgGate · PhilArchive · PhilPapers · Zenodo · Academia.edu

This paper argues that the growing human dependence on AI systems constitutes a philosophically significant violation of two foundational frameworks: Kant's conception of rational autonomy and self-legislation, and Sartre's notion of radical freedom and authentic existence. Where Kant posits that rational beings must be the source of their own moral law, AI dependence risks outsourcing that sovereign capacity. Where Sartre insists on the inescapability of choice as the ground of authentic selfhood, reliance on algorithmic judgment represents a flight from that responsibility.

In Progress Media Ethics

Negativity Bias, Social Media, and Intergenerational Harm to Children's Perception of Reality

2024 · Forthcoming

Drawing on Walter Lippmann's concept of the pseudo-environment and Neil Postman's critique of media's role in shaping consciousness, this paper examines how social media's built-in negativity bias systematically distorts the information environment that children inherit — and how this shapes their foundational beliefs about the world, other people, and possibility itself.
